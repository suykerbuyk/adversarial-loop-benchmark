minor: §3.3 makes a waiter whose own ctx becomes Done during Close's join block on closedCh until every Loader in the cache has returned, including Loaders for unrelated keys. That means Get overruns the caller's deadline by an amount the cache does not control. A prototype of the plan showed this: the waiter's 1s deadline passed and it was still parked until the unrelated slow Loader was released. Nothing in the spec requires it. Rule 7(a) only requires that the waiter returns ErrClosed. The "join before wake" order in rule 8 is about the waiters that Close wakes, and a waiter woken by its own ctx is not one of them. The plan is also inconsistent: §1 has a brand-new Get return ErrClosed at once during the join, yet a waiter that was already parked and whose ctx has fired is held. Recommended: when the wake source was ctx.Done and closed==true, return ErrClosed immediately, and keep the `<-closedCh` wait only for wakes caused by cl.done or closedCh. Alternatively, keep the current behaviour but treat it as a deliberate, documented policy and pin it in test 13. As written, test 13 locks in the overrun.
minor: §3.4 recovers Loader panics and turns them into an error string ("loader panicked: %v"). The spec does not ask for this. It discards the stack trace and turns a programming bug into an ordinary error that callers may retry silently. That makes production failures much harder to diagnose over the years. The usual Go pattern (x/sync/singleflight) is to keep a panic a panic. Recommended: capture debug.Stack() and either re-panic in each waiter's goroutine, or at least include the stack in the error and make the error a distinguishable type. Whichever is chosen, document it as an extension beyond the spec. The Goexit-to-error conversion is fine to keep, because it is what prevents the hang.
nit: errLoaderExited and the panic error are unexported or ad hoc, so callers cannot test for them with errors.Is or errors.As. If the panic and Goexit policy stays, export a sentinel or type. If it is meant to stay unexported, say so in the godoc.
nit: §3.7's code comments number the steps "4. drop" and then "3. wake", the reverse of their order in the code. Renumber them, or merge them into one "3. wake + drop (same critical section)" step, so a later maintainer does not "fix" the order.
nit: Test 11 creates impossible or hand-made internal states (it sets closed=true and closes closedCh directly, then calls wait). That ties the tests to field names and layout. Build these states with a small internal helper, or prefer the end-to-end synctest variants (12, 13), so that refactoring the internals does not mean rewriting the tests.
nit: Test 2's `&r[0]` identity check panics if the Loader payload is empty. Make sure the payload is non-empty, or compare with unsafe.SliceData/cap instead.
nit: Test 22 uses "random keys". Seed it explicitly, or use a deterministic key pattern, so that failures under -count=50 can be reproduced.
nit: In-flight loads are uncapped (one goroutine and one inflight entry per distinct cold key). The spec allows this, since in-flight loads do not count toward capacity, but Loader and Cache godoc should say so, so that operators are not surprised by goroutine growth under a burst of cold keys.
Verification: I built a throwaway prototype of plan §2–§3.7 (since deleted). It passes the public suite under -race -count=20, and go vet is clean. The probes confirmed the plan's key claims: Close parked in loads.Wait is durably blocked under synctest; a concurrent second Close shares the join; Goexit and panic both resolve the flight; Len()==0 after Close; and the waiter gets ErrClosed only after the join. All round-1 findings are addressed by the round-2 plan. I re-derived rules 1–9 against §3, and single-flight, the copy rules, LRU and capacity, orphan loads, close-window discard and the idempotent shared join all hold.
Summary: no blocker or major issues remain. The remaining items are minor or nit: the ctx-expired waiter overrunning its deadline during the join, and the policy of swallowing Loader panics.
