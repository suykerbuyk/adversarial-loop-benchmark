minor: Depth is defined for the stream the parser is in, then the check is written as if `d` were the enclosing Batch. Amendment: top-level stream is depth 0; a Batch's inner sub-stream is one level deeper than the Batch itself; a sub-stream at depth greater than 8 is `ErrCorrupt`; eight nested Batches are legal and a ninth is not. `TestBatchNestedPastDepth8` wraps a Put in 9 Batch frames and requires `ErrCorrupt`. The plan says "When a Batch at depth `d` opens its child stream, that sub-stream is depth `d+1`; reject precisely when the child depth is greater than 8 (`if d+1 > 8`)." That predicate is right only if `d` is the depth of the stream containing the Batch. If the recursive parser's `d` is the stream it is already reading — the natural reading of "stream depth," and what the amendment calls the sub-stream depth — the same `if d+1 > 8` rejects the legal 8-wrap nest (child stream depth 8, `8+1 > 8`) and the matching encode path refuses a stream `Decode` must accept. Pin one convention: either the argument is the enclosing stream depth and the check is `if d+1 > 8` before parsing children, or the argument is already the child stream depth and the check is `if d > 8` on entry, with no second increment. The eight-level accept case in validation does not name which variable is compared.
minor: The hostile-count formula is unsafe on a short Batch payload and is not the check the plan says it is. Rule 4 says before any count-dependent allocation compute `(uint64(len(payload))-4)/9` and reject larger counts, including `0xffffffff`, with no count-sized allocation. `payload` here is the Batch payload after the type byte, so a truncated count header has `len(payload) < 4`. In Go, `uint64(len(payload))-4` wraps; a 0-, 1-, 2-, or 3-byte payload yields a quotient of about `2^64/9`, so `count > capacity` does not fire, and a following `make` keyed off that capacity is itself a huge allocation. Require the four-byte count first (rule 3), and only then compare `uint64(count)` with `(uint64(len(payload))-4)/9`. Also, `(len-4)/9` is a maximum number of minimum-size frames, not a bound that makes `count` safe to use as a slice length: a count equal to that quotient still cannot be materialized when the payload is near 16 MiB (`(16<<20)/9` is about 1.8e6 `Record` headers). The safe rule is the one in the second sentence of rule 4 — append one record per successfully bounded frame, never `make`/`make`-cap from `count` or from that quotient.
minor: "One bounded frame reader and writer" is stated as the structure and then contradicted by the encode path the plan inherits. Rule 2 requires one reader and one writer at all levels so CRC, `len == 0`, the 16 MiB cap, and truncation cannot diverge between outer frames and frames inside a Batch. Rule 6 then says to preserve the v1 concrete type switch (`Put`, non-nil `*Put`, `Delete`, non-nil `*Delete`, plus `Batch` / non-nil `*Batch`). snapshot-v1 `Encode` does not use a shared writer: it special-cases Put/Delete field layout, computes `bodyLen` as `1+4+keyLen` plus optional value, and appends the frame inline. A Kind 3 arm that builds `[count][inner frames]`, wraps with `len`/`crc`, and applies the cap in that arm — while Put/Delete keep the old arithmetic — is two writers. That split is how a Batch frame skips the cap, or a future kind updates only one path. Specify one function that takes an already-built `type+payload`, rejects `len == 0` and `len > uint64(16<<20)`, appends `len`, body, and CRC32-IEEE, and is the only place a frame is emitted, including the outer Batch frame and every nested frame.
